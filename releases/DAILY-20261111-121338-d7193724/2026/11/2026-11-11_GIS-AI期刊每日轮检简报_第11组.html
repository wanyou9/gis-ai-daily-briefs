Residents’ daily consumption (RDC) levels provide an important indicator of regional development. Evidence linking neighbourhood green space with obesity remains inconsistent, partly because previous studies have often treated green space as a homogeneous environmental exposure and overlooked differences in vegetation composition, spatial context, and population characteristics. This study examined the relative predictive importance of neighbourhood green space characteristics for overweight/obesity status according to spatial location, vegetation type, and age group. We analysed questionnaire data from 789 residents across 12 neighbourhoods in Suzhou, China. Eye-level green-space exposure was quantified using street view imagery (SVI) and deep-learning semantic segmentation to distinguish overall greenery, grass, and tree cover within and outside neighbourhood boundaries. XGBoost models combined with SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) were applied to evaluate the relative predictive importance of different green space characteristics in predicting overweight/obesity status. Results showed that green spaces within neighbourhood boundaries had greater relative predictive importance than green spaces outside neighbourhood boundaries. The Green View Index (GVI) within neighbourhoods was the most influential environmental indicator contributing to lower predicted probabilities of overweight/obesity at higher GVI values. Among vegetation types, neighbourhood grass had the greatest relative predictive importance, whereas tree cover was less important overall. Tree cover had greater relative predictive importance than grass within the model fitted to older residents, whereas grass had the highest relative predictive importance within the younger-resident model. These findings indicate that the predictive relevance of neighbourhood green space depends not only on its spatial location and vegetation composition, but also on the population group considered. Neighbourhood green space planning that considers the location and composition of green space, together with the needs of different population groups, may support healthier and more equitable urban environments.

期刊：Knowledge and Information Systems · DOI：10.1007/s10115-026-02911-1 · 摘要来源：已核验来源

Industrial textile manufacturing requires decision support systems that not only estimate quality-related risk but also translate model outputs into interpretable and operationally meaningful decision support information. This study proposes a leakage-aware and artifact-grounded framework for explainable risk prediction and model-consistent decision support in textile production. A transparent 0–100 CompositeRiskScore is defined as a retrospective quality risk proxy based on quality, meter difference, downtime, and production duration components. For prediction, only pre-QC production, planning, and process descriptors are used as input variables. The proposed framework integrates local explanations, Soft Direction-Consistency filtering, train-only low-risk reference bands, model-consistent what-if checks, and a locked context retrieval interface over structured JSON artifacts into a unified decision support workflow. Rather than treating model attributions as direct prescriptions, the explanation layer organizes them into recommendation candidates with explicit actionability levels, including real-time adjustable, setup-level adjustable, planning/procurement-level adjustable, and contextual feature groups. Candidate changes are evaluated using model-consistent what-if checks that measure whether the proposed changes reduce the model-predicted CompositeRiskScore. These checks quantify internal trained model response and are not evidence that applying the changes will improve realized production quality. The main evaluation uses a roll-group-disjoint split to prevent analytical rows linked to the same roll-level production group from crossing the Train, Validation, and Test partitions. Under this evaluation setting, the model achieves more moderate but meaningful risk-ranking performance, while unseen pattern and unseen loom scenarios remain more challenging generalization settings. The retrieval layer is designed as a structured decision support interface rather than an open-ended RAG reasoning system. It uses a single primary context, preserves locked prediction and explanation fields, excludes post-QC and ground-truth information from deployment-facing answers, and returns an explicit “not available in context” fallback when requested information is absent from the selected context. The results show that the proposed framework can transform leakage-controlled risk predictions into traceable explanation, recommendation, and retrieval artifacts.

Multi-vector dense retrieval methods have set the state-of-the-art retrieval quality on ranking tasks. However, the superior effectiveness of such methods comes with substantial computational overhead, which makes their deployment in large-scale search engines infeasible. As a remedy, we propose a novel approach, Sparcent, that leverages learned sparse retrieval (LSR) to accelerate the query-centroid matching stage in multi-vector dense retrieval. Specifically, we first derive sparse centroid representations for multi-vector term-embedding clusters and then prune them, producing compact centroid vectors that remain both efficient and effective for the indexing via LSR methods. Next, we integrate Sparcent with two well-known multi-vector retrieval systems, PLAID and WARP, already optimized for single-core CPU search. Our experimental results show that Sparcent provides up to 4.6× speed-up over the original PLAID and 1.3× speed-up over WARP, respectively, with virtually zero additional memory, while maintaining result effectiveness on both in-domain and out-of-domain retrieval tasks.
