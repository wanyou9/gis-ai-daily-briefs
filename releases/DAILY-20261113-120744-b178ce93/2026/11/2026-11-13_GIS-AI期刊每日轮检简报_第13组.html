论文题名

Age-Period-Cohort Analysis of the Global Death Burden of Main Diseases Attributable to High Temperature in the Past 30 Years: Based on the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021

期刊：GeoHealth · DOI：10.1029/2025gh001623 · 语言：en · 摘要来源：Crossref 元数据

High temperatures pose a serious threat to human health. Quantifying the main disease death burden attributable to high temperatures can provide scientific evidence for policy development. Using Global Burden of Disease 2021 data, we identified the death burden attributable to high temperatures across all causes and determined the three leading causes. For each, we used age‐standardized mortality rates attributable to high temperature (HA‐ASMR) and estimated temporal trends via age‐period‐cohort modeling at global, regional, and national levels (1990–2021). Ischemic heart disease, stroke, and lower respiratory infections were the top three causes of deaths attributable to high temperature in 2021. Low‐middle Socio‐demographic Index (SDI) regions had the highest HA‐ASMR for ischemic heart disease (3.49, 95%UI: 0.96–6.57) and stroke (2.55, 95%UI: 0.77–5.01), whereas lower respiratory infections showed the highest HA‐ASMR in low SDI regions (2.52, 95%UI: 0.85–4.35). Age‐period‐cohort analysis generated net drift values for HA‐ASMR in high SDI regions, with values of 0.99% (95%CI: 0.60%–1.39%) for ischemic heart disease, 1.19% (95%CI: 0.99%–1.40%) for stroke, and 2.36% (95%CI: 2.01%–2.70%) for lower respiratory infections. Longitudinal age curves showed elevated mortality risks attributable to high temperature in populations over 60 years. Global period and cohort rate ratios for high‐temperature‐attributable ischemic heart disease mortality increased over time. Global ischemic heart disease and stroke deaths attributable to high temperature continued rising with sharply elevated risks in older adults, while lower respiratory infection deaths attributable to high temperature among youth in high‐income countries are increasing. Therefore, adaptation strategies must be specific to disease and age, varying by regional characteristics.

期刊：GeoHealth · DOI：10.1029/2026gh001884 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Atmospheric fine particulate matter (PM2.5) is one of the most important risk factors for various respiratory and cardiovascular diseases. We develop a two-phase data-driven framework that uses geostationary Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) retrievals to predict hourly PM2.5 at 0.01° × 0.01° resolution and support exposure assessment across multiple spatial and temporal contexts. We use a U-Net-like partial convolutional neural network framework inspired by image inpainting to address missing Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) data from the GOES-R geostationary satellite. To fill large spatiotemporal gaps in the GOES-R record, we implement an iterative gap-filling approach that expands contextual information for successive predictions, maintaining strong agreement with collocated AERONET observations (R = 0.75) while increasing paired observations from 430,603 to 728,115. A random forest is used to predict surface PM2.5 concentrations using our spatiotemporally contiguous gap-filled AOD. The resulting PM2.5 estimates over 2019–2023 in the top 50 most populous counties over the CONUS capture the magnitude and spatial gradient as measured by the EPA Air Quality System (AQS) and PurpleAir low-cost sensor network (daily R² = 0.85, mean bias = −0.11 μg/m³). Additional wildfire case studies in less densely monitored rural regions demonstrate the framework's utility for characterizing short-term exposure events despite substantial gaps in satellite observations. These findings highlight the value of gap-filled geostationary AOD for high-resolution exposure assessment.

Global Health Hazards From Human-Derived Volatile Organic Compounds Amid Emission Variability

期刊：GeoHealth · DOI：10.1029/2026gh001910 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Health risks from high air pollution exposure remain challenging despite pollution control strategies. Health hazards caused by particulate matter and ozone have been reported for the past three decades. Here, we report the hazard quotient for Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs) causing non-carcinogenic risk globally using chemistry-climate model simulations. We report hazard quotient (HQ) for three scenarios: (a) baseline scenario, (b) continued increasing emissions leading to doubling of anthropogenic VOCs (DVOCS), and (c) mitigation scenarios where emissions are reduced to half (HalfVOCS). Interestingly, we report that long-term ambient exposure to VOCs increases non-carcinogenic health risk by up to 36% in the high-emission scenario. Hazardous VOCs such as benzene, formaldehyde, hydrogen cyanide, and acetonitrile remain at levels that pose chronic risks to large populations. Benzene and formaldehyde pose the greatest hazards, with maximum HQ ranging from 1.4 to 2.0. Additionally, the DVOCS scenario increases the risk to the exposed population by 18.6%–21.5% in northern India and eastern China, and by 10.7% in West-Central Africa. Conversely, the HalfVOCS scenario shows a decrease in the exposed population of −3 to −21%. We estimate that approximately a million people face chronic respiratory and neurological risks from current ambient VOCs levels. Our findings demonstrate that VOCs exposure constitutes a major driver of the global disease burden, necessitating immediate international emission-reduction strategies.

A Spatiotemporal Modeling Approach for Predicting Concentrations of Ammonia in an Agricultural Region

期刊：GeoHealth · DOI：10.1029/2025gh001517 · 语言：en · 摘要来源：Crossref 元数据

The availability of data on ambient ammonia exposure is limited in both spatial and temporal resolutions, especially in rural areas, despite its potential health impacts and rapidly increasing emissions. In this study, a land‐use regression (LUR)‐based spatiotemporal modeling approach was applied to predict agricultural‐related ammonia concentrations at a fine spatial scale, extending the method's application in terms of the species of pollutant and the type of region. In this approach, spatiotemporal variations are primarily explained by a linear combination of temporal basis functions, with weights determined by LUR. We developed the model using ammonia concentrations we collected in 2022 and 2023 from 10 monitoring sites within Urbana‐Champaign, Illinois, along with geographic covariates focused on agricultural features. Analysis of the model structure showed that livestock farms and populated areas are associated with ammonia peaks in summer and winter, whereas croplands are more closely associated with peaks in spring and fall. Cross‐validated predictions showed high accuracies at most monitoring sites ( R 2 ≥ 0.69 at 90% of the sites), though accuracy varied by location, with lower accuracy linked to unique time trend in ammonia concentrations at a location. Finally, we mapped the seasonal predictions of ammonia concentrations across the study area at 30‐m spatial resolution. In contrast, alternative models using simpler formulation or a smaller selection of covariates could not achieve an acceptable level of performance in identical evaluations. These results demonstrate the potential of LUR‐based models as valuable tools for investigating agriculture‐related air pollution exposure and highlight their broader applicability.

Childhood Lead-Exposure Prevention Through Predictive Mapping of At-Risk Housing Nationwide: A Free, Public-Data Screening Map Checked Against Measured Blood Lead in 11 States

期刊：GeoHealth · DOI：10.1029/2026gh002053 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Childhood lead exposure causes irreversible neurodevelopmental harm, and no safe blood lead level in children has been identified. Measured childhood blood lead is collected unevenly across the United States, and many areas publish no neighborhood-level surveillance, so the children at highest risk are often the hardest to find by measurement. Building on the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency finding that housing age and poverty predict where childhood lead exposure concentrates, we computed a lead-exposure risk score for all 83,388 U.S. census tracts and 3,222 counties from public American Community Survey housing-age and poverty data, and tested it against measured childhood blood lead in 11 states by Spearman rank correlation. On surveillance the source study never used, predicted risk tracked measured elevated blood lead at rho = 0.64 across Wisconsin's 1,308 census tracts, with five independent county-scale states ranging from 0.48 to 0.77 and three further independent tract states (Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Minnesota) agreeing. A flexible model on the same public inputs explains only about half the tract-level variance, so housing age and poverty leave most of the variation unexplained, and the map is a screening signal rather than a diagnosis. Every validation state is an older-housing Northeast, Midwest, or Great Plains jurisdiction, so the low-risk predictions for the newer-built West and Sun Belt remain an untested extrapolation. Reproducible from public data and free to use, the map ranks where confirmatory testing should go first, including in states that publish no surveillance of their own.

期刊：GeoHealth · DOI：10.1029/2026gh001874 · 语言：en · 摘要来源：Crossref 元数据

Young children (birth to age five) are uniquely vulnerable to environmental hazards due to their higher exposure relative to body weight, rapid physiological and neurological development, and strong reliance on caregivers for protection and care. Such risks are often amplified in marginalized communities with socioeconomic disadvantage and limited access to resources. However, widely used indices, such as the Social Vulnerability Index (SVI), the Climate Vulnerability Index (CVI), and the Child Opportunity Index (COI), were not specifically developed for young children and may not capture the combined environmental and socioeconomic risks faced by this age group. To address this critical gap, we developed a county‐level Early Childhood Environmental Health Vulnerability Index (EC‐EHVI) for the contiguous US using multidimensional indicators within an Exposure‐Sensitivity‐Adaptive Capacity framework and informed by Bronfenbrenner's bioecological model. We identified the underlying drivers and the spatial patterns of the EC‐EHVI. Our results showed that the EC‐EHVI exhibited the strongest association with county‐level mortality among young children and explained a larger proportion of spatial heterogeneity compared with the SVI, CVI, and COI. Elevated vulnerability clustered in the Great Plains and Southeastern US, where over half of high‐vulnerability counties were exposure‐driven, and 411 high–high hotspots were identified. The EC‐EHVI offers a valuable spatial decision‐support tool for designing targeted, place‐based interventions and advancing environmental health equity for young children.

Compound Heatwaves and Health Inequality: A Nationwide Study on Emergency Admissions and Socioeconomic Vulnerability in South Korea

期刊：GeoHealth · DOI：10.1029/2026gh001920 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Climate change is intensifying the frequency and severity of extreme heat events. Despite the rising frequency of compound heatwaves (CHWs)—concurrent daytime and nighttime heat—evidence regarding cause-specific risks and socioeconomic disparities remains limited. This study aimed to examine associations between CHWs and emergency hospital admissions and to identify vulnerable subpopulations. This study analyzed 2,010,769 emergency hospital admissions in the summer (June–September, 2011–2019) using the South Korean National Health Insurance Service database. Heatwaves were classified as daytime, nighttime, or compound based on district-specific 90th percentile thresholds of the daily maximum and minimum temperatures. A time-stratified case-crossover design with a distributed lag model was applied to estimate odds ratios for emergency hospital admissions associated with each heatwave type. Subgroup analyses were conducted including age, sex, household income, population density, and admission cause. Furthermore, we tested the potential impacts of income by incorporating an interaction term between income and each subgroup. CHWs were associated with emergency admissions, with particularly strong associations for genitourinary diseases. The risks were highest among the elderly, male, residents of highly populated areas, and low-income groups. Furthermore, the association was most pronounced for genitourinary diseases, where the low-income group showed the highest risk. Daytime heatwaves showed higher risks than nighttime heatwaves, which had weaker but disease-specific associations. This study showed the health risks of CHWs that disproportionately affect genitourinary conditions and low-income populations, emphasizing the need for climate adaptation planning that prioritize vulnerable groups and heat-sensitive diseases.

Extreme heat is intensifying under climate change and poses disproportionate risks to children, yet Canadian children’s lifetime exposure to rare, high‐severity heatwaves remains poorly quantified at high spatial resolution. We estimate lifetime heatwave exposure across Canada under four local warming categories using bias‐corrected, downscaled CMIP6 projections aggregated to 57,932 census areas and linked with age‐structured population data. Heatwave severity is measured using the Heat Wave Magnitude Index daily (HWMId), which combines intensity and duration. A rare, high‐severity heatwave is defined as a year in which HWMId exceeds the local 98th percentile during 1981–2010—an event historically expected less than once in an average lifetime. Projected exposure increases sharply and nonlinearly with warming. Mean lifetime exposure rises from 0.44 events under 1–3°C of local warming to 4.77 under 3–5°C, 17.67 under 5–7°C, and 39.45 under 7+°C—an 89‐fold increase. Repeated exposure escalates even faster: no children experience five or more events under 1–3°C, compared with 39.4% under 3–5°C and 87.3% under 5–7°C. Exposure emerges earliest in western provinces, while eastern and northern regions reach similarly widespread exposure as warming increases. These findings show that historically exceptional heatwaves are projected to become recurrent over Canadian children’s lifetimes and provide spatially explicit evidence for child‐centered adaptation, including cooling infrastructure, heat‐health early warning systems, and pediatric preparedness.

Intra‐day TV was positively associated with all‐causes, cardiovascular, and respiratory mortality, with the strongest association observed for respiratory mortality. Incorporating intra‐day TV into early warning systems may help reduce the mortality burden.
