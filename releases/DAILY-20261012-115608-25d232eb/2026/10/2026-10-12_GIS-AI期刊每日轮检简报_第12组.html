论文题名

GeoAI research in transition: thematic structures, temporal evolution and method–domain linkages

期刊：International Journal of Geographical Information Science · DOI：10.1080/13658816.2026.2617348 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Geospatial artificial intelligence (GeoAI) has emerged at the intersection of artificial intelligence and geospatial science, advancing applications in environmental monitoring, urban analytics and methodological innovation. Existing reviews are limited: bibliometric analyses rely on metadata such as keywords and citations, while narrative syntheses focus on narrow subdomains. These approaches provide little semantic depth, capture limited temporal dynamics and seldom link computational methods to application domains. This study applies full-text structural topic modeling (STM) to over 100 peer-reviewed publications, enabling semantic-level theme extraction, temporal trend analysis and systematic mapping of methods to domains. Findings reveal three main insights. First, GeoAI research clusters into three strands: environmental applications (soil, groundwater, floods, air quality), urban and human-centered analytics (vegetation, walkability, safety) and methodological frontiers (foundation models, generative AI, graph neural networks, explainable AI). Second, temporal analysis shows a transition from early emphases on remote sensing and infrastructures toward socially embedded, sustainability-oriented themes, including climate resilience and public health. Third, method–domain mappings indicate both specialization (e.g. CNNs in imagery, ensembles in environmental modeling) and diffusion across domains (e.g. transformers, explainable AI). Overall, this study delivers the first full-text STM-based synthesis of GeoAI, offering a roadmap for aligning AI innovation with sustainability and urban governance challenges.

论文题名

Sounds in the city: on the utility of acoustic landmarks

期刊：International Journal of Geographical Information Science · DOI：10.1080/13658816.2026.2617940 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Most navigation systems rely heavily on visual landmarks like buildings and bridges, while largely overlooking acoustic landmarks. Research has primarily focused on visual landmarks in real-world environments and on acoustic landmarks in virtual settings, leaving a gap in understanding the potential of acoustic landmarks for real-world navigation. This study investigates the role of urban sounds as potential acoustic landmarks for pedestrian navigation. Eighteen participants completed a route-following task in an urban environment and evaluated a range of characteristic sounds in terms of how identifiable, audible, and helpful they are for navigation. Results show that certain sounds – such as auditory traffic lights for the blind and traffic-related sounds – were perceived as particularly helpful and useful. In contrast, sounds from conversations and fans were less audible and less helpful for navigation. Our findings suggest that incorporating select acoustic landmarks into navigation systems can be helpful and support wayfinding for users, noting that in this study, participants were deliberately limited to non-visual landmarks.

MAPLID: a new multi-label approach for place identification using data supplied by mobile network operators

期刊：International Journal of Geographical Information Science · DOI：10.1080/13658816.2026.2617932 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Mobile phone data provide valuable insights into urban structure, yet traditional clustering methods assign a single label (e.g., home or work) per location, oversimplifying mixed-use areas. We present MAPLID, a supervised multi-label framework for place identification using Call Detail Records (CDRs) from Telecom Italia. The study area in Milan (23.5 𝑘⁢𝑚2) was divided into 10,000 grid cells, with a 20 × 20 subgrid (4.7 𝑘⁢𝑚2) manually labelled for home, work, and high/medium/low density. The best performance, achieving 88.3% average precision and leading in four of nine metrics, was achieved with Label Powerset and Random Forest applied to outgoing call data. MAPLID was further validated in the province of Trento, confirming its robustness in more irregular and forested terrain. Incorporating multimodal Point of Interest (POI) features from OpenStreetMap improved accuracy in data-sparse regions like Trento. The results demonstrate MAPLID’s capability to capture multiple concurrent urban functions and to generalise across contrasting geographic contexts, offering a computationally efficient framework for dynamic land-use mapping, urban planning, and mobility analysis using large-scale telecom data.

RiverLakeBasins: a global dataset of nested river-watersheds and lake-hillslopes

期刊：International Journal of Geographical Information Science · DOI：10.1080/13658816.2026.2620024 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Accurate delineation of watershed boundaries is fundamental for hydrological modeling, water resource management, and Earth system research. However, most global watershed datasets focus primarily on river networks while overlooking the critical role of lakes, which can significantly alter flow paths, watershed boundaries, and hydrological connectivity. This study introduces RiverLakeBasins, a global-scale nested watershed dataset that explicitly integrates lakes into a hierarchical delineation framework by refining and extending the Pfafstetter coding system. Using the MERIT Hydro and HydroLAKES datasets, we simultaneously delineate river-watersheds and lake drainage areas (lake-hillslopes) across multiple spatial levels. We develop reproducible algorithms to optimize stream networks, delineate lake-hillslopes, and reconstruct upstream–downstream flow relationships among rivers, lakes, and lake-hillslopes. Validation against benchmark datasets shows high spatial and topological accuracy: for 97% of shared lakes, the upstream watershed area differs by <5% relative to Lake-TopoCat. On the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau, RiverLakeBasins identifies endorheic lakes with 97.4% accuracy and exorheic lakes with 99.1%, outperforming HydroBASINS. Representative case studies demonstrate improved lake–river connectivity in complex landscapes. By explicitly incorporating lakes and preserving scale-consistent connectivity, RiverLakeBasins provides a global, multi-level dataset to support hydrological modeling, runoff simulation, aquatic ecosystem research, and integrated water resource management.

This study explores Quantitative and Qualitative Spatial Pattern Matching (QQ-SPM) queries, and showcase their applicability in Point of Interest (POI) search scenarios. This query format allows the search for groups of closely-located and relevant geo-textual objects based on a spatial pattern graph with keywords, pairwise distance limits, and topological constraints. We propose the Quantitative and Qualitative Efficient Spatial Pattern Matching Generalized Solution (QQESPM-GS), an approach designed to efficiently process such queries across different geospatial technologies. Experiments with a real POI dataset from UK, show that our approach remains robust with increasing dataset sizes and query constraints. Additionally, it achieves at least one order of magnitude faster query times compared to baseline methods for solving QQ-SPM queries.

Geo-SAM: a structure-attribute match method for geographic data prediction

期刊：International Journal of Geographical Information Science · DOI：10.1080/13658816.2026.2622386 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Spatial prediction aims to estimate values at unobserved locations by leveraging spatial correlations and feature dependencies. While graph neural networks (GNNs) have shown strong performance in this domain by encoding spatial proximity through graph construction and embedding node attributes, they often struggle with data that are sparse, heterogeneous, or complex. As such, we proposed the Structure-Attribute Matching (Geo-SAM) model, a novel framework that decoupled and realigned spatial and attribute information through dual-path encoding and statistical alignment. Geo-SAM employed a GNN-based structural encoder that captured spatial correlations and a neural network (NN)-based attribute encoder that processed non-spatial features. Their latent representations were aligned using Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) to ensure coherence between spatial and attribute representations. To mitigate the over-smoothing, a known limitation of deep GNNs, residual connections were integrated into both encoders to retain discriminative signals. The aligned latent representations were subsequently fused and decoded to generate predictions. An adaptive loss-balancing mechanism dynamically adjusted the weight between reconstruction and alignment losses, facilitating stable optimization across varying data conditions. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrated that Geo-SAM consistently outperforms representative baselines, delivering state-of-the-art accuracy and robustness across diverse spatial prediction scenarios.

期刊：International Journal of Geographical Information Science · DOI：10.1080/13658816.2026.2623155 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Multiscale geographically and temporally weighted regression (MGTWR) is widely applied to address spatiotemporal heterogeneity and relationships at different scales in domains such as land use, urban vitality, and transportation. However, conventional MGTWR calibration, relying on locally weighted least squares and the back-fitting algorithm, faces two key limitations: boundary effects (i.e. edge bias) and high computational cost. To address these issues, this study proposes an efficient calibration algorithm for MGTWR, termed the Two-Step Calibration Algorithm based on local linear fitting (MGTWR_2SCALL). By exploiting the local linear structure via Taylor series expansion, MGTWR_2SCALL performs model calibration through a two-stage local smoothing procedure, eliminating the need for the iterative back-fitting process. The algorithm’s performance is rigorously evaluated through simulation experiments and a real-world case study analyzing carbon emissions in China during 2014–2021. The results demonstrate that MGTWR_2SCALL effectively mitigates boundary effects and enhances computational efficiency. Thus, MGTWR_2SCALL offers substantial theoretical and practical significance for advancing spatiotemporal statistical modeling.

High-performance cluster infrastructure supplies essential computational capabilities for large-scale geocomputation. However, many parallel geocomputation tasks are impacted by the heterogeneity of geospatial data, particularly when complex spatial structures or uneven feature distributions cause spatially uneven computational workloads across data partitions. This uneven cost is commonly described as computational intensity (CI), which reflects the amount of computation required per data unit and plays a critical role in data partitioning for parallel geocomputation. Existing CI estimation methods usually rely on expert-driven heuristics or simplified statistical indicators, which struggle to generalize across different parallel geocomputation tasks. As a result, inaccurate CI prediction leads to load imbalance and degraded parallel performance. To address those issues, the paper proposes a Learning-based Computing Framework for Geospatial data (LCF-G) to predict CI and guide parallel geocomputation. In LCF-G, geospatial data are represented as graphs to capture spatial relationships and organizational patterns influencing computational cost. A Graph Convolutional Network regression model for Geospatial data (GCN4G), is proposed to learn the mapping between graph-structured data characteristics and CI. Experiments on three representative geocomputation tasks show that GCN4G achieves an R-squared (R²) value above 0.8 in CI prediction, while LCF-G improves parallel efficiency to over 0.8, outperforming existing approaches.

期刊：International Journal of Geographical Information Science · DOI：10.1080/13658816.2026.2624786 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Regular-grid Digital Elevation Model (DEM) data are fundamental for viewshed analysis, with direct effects on both computational accuracy and efficiency. High-resolution DEMs yield more detailed results, but computational costs can be prohibitively high, creating a dilemma of balancing computational accuracy and efficiency. To address this, we propose a multi-resolution viewshed (MRVd) method that couples varying resolutions of terrain data based on distance from the viewpoint: high-resolution DEM data are used near the viewpoint, while low-resolution DEM data are used further away. Experiments conducted across mountainous, plain, and urban terrains show that MRVd can achieve better performance than fixed-resolution viewshed approaches with only marginal additional computation time. Accuracy improvements were 2–3 times on average and up to 6–7 times in some cases. Furthermore, MRVd effectively mitigates chunk distortion by leveraging the localized high-resolution DEM data, producing results similar to those obtained using uniformly high-resolution DEM data. These findings suggest that coupling multi-resolution terrain data provides a scalable, resource-efficient computational strategy to balance accuracy and efficiency in viewshed computation. The MRVd approach enhances the robustness and reliability of viewshed calculation for practical applications in landscape assessment, spatial planning, archaeology, and related fields.

论文题名

Hoeffding adaptive splitting trees for data stream classification with concept drift and ensemble learning

期刊：Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery · DOI：10.1007/s10618-026-01255-2 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Ensembles of decision trees are well-established methods for data stream classification. In ensemble learning, Hoeffding Trees are widely adopted as base learners, performing periodic split attempts according to the Hoeffding bound. Recent studies, however, indicate that this standard splitting mechanism lacks adaptability, while adaptive trees that trigger splits in response to performance degradation have achieved superior results. In this paper, we identify limitations in the use of adaptive-splitting decision trees as ensemble base learners, showing that change detectors often fail to promote sufficient diversity within ensembles. To address this issue, we propose two novel decision tree models, termed Hoeffding Adaptive Splitting Trees. These models combine the periodic splitting strategy of Hoeffding Trees, which fosters ensemble diversity, with adaptive splitting mechanisms that employ change detection algorithms to identify performance decay and determine split points. Experimental results demonstrate that Hoeffding Adaptive Splitting Trees enhance ensemble performance and achieve state-of-the-art results across a comprehensive evaluation, including benchmark comparisons, computational cost analysis, and concept drift adaptation.

论文题名

Evolving digital skill demands before and after generative AI: a global network analysis via upwork data

期刊：Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery · DOI：10.1007/s10618-026-01271-2 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

A human resource gap has emerged due to the rapid advancement of technology. Companies are attempting to address this issue by either developing their own resources (such as staff training or new recruitment) or utilizing external resources. Consequently, freelance professionals are increasingly being used to support different phases of projects in hourly or project-based work opportunities. Consequently, job postings from various regions around the world are connecting with freelancers globally. How should individuals, who constitute the foundation of the digital economy, respond to the evolving needs of companies in Europe, Asia, Africa and the Americas? How should educational institutions train the human resources required by this sector? This study aims to generate a skills-based network in the digital labor market to guide both employers and employees in their career choices. Specifically, the study aims to identify changes in skill demand between 2022 and 2025. Furthermore, the study focuses on determining the position of Generative AI (GenAI) within the digital skill demand network, given its widespread adoption in 2023. The findings show that the number of common skills across continents dropped from 328 in 2022 to 181 in 2025, and that GenAI is most prominently connected to visual communication and web development clusters, with distinct continental patterns. The study also aims to computationally interpret GenAI tool usage patterns both globally and across different continents. By doing so, the study will offer valuable insights to career planners, educators and employers, helping them to make strategic decisions.

Rethinking neighbourhood boundaries for urban planning: a data-driven framework for perception-based delineation

期刊：International Journal of Geographical Information Science · DOI：10.1080/13658816.2026.2739884 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Neighbourhoods are fundamental units for urban analysis, contextualising social and economic life. However, research often relies on administrative geographies as proxies, compromising meaningful delineation for data accessibility. This is a critical problem as administrative boundaries frequently mask significant internal heterogeneity by grouping distinct areas, which can lead to misinterpretations of local conditions and social inequality. This research presents a novel methodology for delineating more homogeneous neighbourhood units based on a large-scale mapping of the urban environment’s visual perception. A deep learning model, trained on street view images and associated perception data, was used to construct a city-wide perception profile of Glasgow, UK. Subsequently, spatial clustering techniques were used to delineate ‘perceptual neighbourhood’ based on shared visual and perceptual characteristics. When compared against standard census geographies using the V-measure, these data-driven neighbourhoods exhibited only moderate spatial association. The results indicate that official census zones, despite their intended socio-economic homogeneity, are often comprised of multiple, distinct perceptual areas. This work provides a scalable, replicable method for generating internally homogeneous, human-centred neighbourhood units. This approach can improve our understanding of the relationship between place and social inequality and inform more targeted, evidence-based urban policy.

论文题名

Balancing factoid retrieval and candidate recommendation to identify toponym origins in gazetteers

期刊：International Journal of Geographical Information Science · DOI：10.1080/13658816.2026.2733533 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

This paper investigates the adaptation of large language models (LLMs) for gazetteer enrichment, specifically to resolve missing placename origins in the Melbourne gazetteer (Australia). Gazetteers are foundational databases for geographic information systems. However, querying toponym etymologies remains challenging: populating such attributes resists rule-based inference and necessitates direct semantic retrieval from unstructured resources. We adopted two search strategies: factoid retrieval for explicit origins, and candidate recommendation otherwise. The use of nonspatial models risks degraded spatial matching performance. Hence, we developed a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) architecture within a geographic question-answering framework. Our system employed parallel semantic-spatial rankers that deliberately enforced a geographic bias toward target regions. Our system improved spatial ranking scores by up to 7% in normalized discounted cumulative gain. However, at the final stage, LLMs still struggled to reliably filter spatial matches. To investigate this limitation, we conducted a probing experiment on standalone LLMs. We revealed intrinsic spatial distortions regarding adjacency relations in Greater Melbourne, notably an inherent popularity bias that prevented fine-grained responses. Our findings suggest that effectively deploying LLMs for spatial data origins must advance beyond factoid retrieval by also refining candidate recommendation at every stage: calibrated biases adapt language model search to local specificities.

A high-resolution civil aviation emission dataset in China based on real-world flight trajectories

期刊：Big Earth Data · DOI：10.1080/20964471.2026.2735639 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Aviation emissions impose substantial pressure on the environment, necessitating the development of effective mitigation strategies. Effective emission reduction policies urgently require fine-grained aviation emissions data. The spatiotemporal resolutions of existing aviation emission datasets are largely confined to annual or monthly at the global or national scale, as shown in the EDGAR and IPCC reports, whereas fine-grained aviation emission datasets remain scarce. In this study, a technical framework is proposed to estimate instant emissions along real flight trajectories using Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast (ADS-B) data. Then, a daily aviation emission dataset in China is built that depicts carbon dioxide (CO2), nitrogen oxides (NOₓ), carbon monoxide (CO), and hydrocarbon (HC) emissions generated by all civil aviation activities. With the generated dataset, the emission characteristics considering the variation in aviation phases were analyzed, which were then aggregated to the corresponding airports, routes, and airlines. It is observed that average daily emissions of the civil aviation activities amount to 0.27Mt CO2, 1.34kt NOₓ, 0.23kt CO, and 0.02kt HC, respectively. The dataset reveals the heterogeneous spatiotemporal distribution of aviation emissions within different time periods across various regions. This moves beyond simple spatial mapping to assess airline environmental performance, manage airport air quality, and optimize flight routes.

论文题名

SAGE-Loop: a plug-and-play semantic evolution framework for enhancing neural reconstruction from crowdsourced street view videos

期刊：Big Earth Data · DOI：10.1080/20964471.2026.2728212 · 摘要来源：出版方摘要

Crowdsourced Street View Videos (CSVVs) offer a highly scalable data source for 3D urban reconstruction. However, neural rendering algorithms (e.g., 3DGS, NeRF) strictly demand dense multi-view constraints. When processing the sparse, unidirectional trajectories of CSVVs, these algorithms suffer from significant degradation, producing severe geometric floaters and structural blurs. While generative Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) can effectively infer these missing urban details due to the strong semantic continuity of physical environments, unconstrained generative priors lack 3D awareness, leading to multi-view hallucinations. To resolve this, we propose SAGE-Loop, a self-supervised framework that utilizes the 3D manifold as a “geometric sieve” to validate 2D generative priors. The system employs a Progressive Virtual Trajectory Evolution (PVTE) strategy to explicitly synthesize novel viewpoints, deliberately exposing hidden rendering defects. An LMM then generates semantically coherent image patches to replace these localized artifacts. Crucially, a Residual Convergence-based rollback mechanism tests these generative updates against the established 3D geometry, discarding physically inconsistent hallucinations. Experiments on the CS3D-Continuous dataset demonstrate that SAGE-Loop achieves a 2.69 dB PSNR gain over vanilla 3DGS and reduces the Artifact Pixel Ratio to 1.32%, successfully transforming fragmented crowdsourced observations into reliable 3D GIS assets.
