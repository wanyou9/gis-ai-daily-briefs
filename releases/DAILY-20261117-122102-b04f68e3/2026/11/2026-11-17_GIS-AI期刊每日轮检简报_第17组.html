Smart environments and integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) are steering 6G from “signal transmission" toward “collaborative environmental perception and proactive control". Unlike conventional designs that treat the environment as an external random factor, smart environments leverage programmable metasurfaces, intelligent transmissive and reflective surfaces, vehicle-mounted surfaces, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and other autonomous platforms, reconfigurable antennas, and multi-base-station cooperative networks to transform propagation paths, array geometries, coverage topologies, and node cooperation into optimizable degrees of freedom. Finally, key challenges in typical scenarios, including vehicle-to-everything (V2X), industrial manufacturing, indoor spaces, and integrated space-air-ground networks, are summarized, along with promising directions for future research.

Satellite systems supporting low-Earth-orbit networking, distributed Earth observation, and onboard intelligence are evolving from fixed configurations and predefined functional assignments toward task-driven multi-satellite cooperation. This survey develops a system-level framework for communication, sensing, and computing in reconfigurable satellite swarms. It first distinguishes reconfigurable swarms from fixed constellations, formation flying, fractionated spacecraft, software-defined satellites, and reconfigurable antennas. Directly adjustable system objects are organized into four coupled dimensions: formation, link, function, and resource reconfiguration. A four-layer operating architecture then connects state awareness, network organization, task execution, and reconfiguration decisions. For finite task windows, the survey introduces a net-benefit evaluation principle based on predeclared and well-tuned baselines, separate cost budgets, hard feasibility constraints, risk bounds, and task-level outcomes. The resulting synthesis clarifies when reconfiguration is worthwhile, which system objects should be adjusted, and how its performance boundaries can be established using reproducible evidence.

期刊：Big Data · DOI：10.1177/2167647x261502269 · 摘要来源：Crossref 元数据

In the evolving landscape of advancements cities are developing rapidly. To meet basic needs people are increasingly using personal vehicles, which increases traffic and the complexity of driving. The primary goal of this research is to ingest, store, and analyze the traffic dataset using a novel technique. While surveying the existing methods it was found that data ingestion and management techniques were quite limited; the existing studies primarily focused on analyzing data using deep network architectures, which require higher computational resources due to their complex architecture. To overcome these gaps we proposed the incremental histogram and quantile partition as quantum approximate optimization algorithm (IHQ-QAOA) technique, which utilizes histogram binning and quantile techniques to ingest data more efficiently with minimal resources. The QAOA technique addresses the clusters by optimizing the cost and identifying the lane change behavior of the vehicles. We extracted insights to analyze traffic patterns using online analytical processing techniques (pivoting, drill-through, slicing, and dicing), which supported the segregation of features from section to lane level, resulting in better data visualization. The proposed techniques are compared with the state-of-the-art using the Next Generation SIMulation (NGSIM) and New Era of Urban Traffic Monitoring with Aerial footage (pNEUMA) datasets, resulting in an average data ingestion efficiency of 75.53%; for lane change analysis, the pNEUMA dataset was tested on a model trained on the NGSIM dataset, and overall accuracies of more than 0.96 were obtained. These achievements show that the suggested approach is reliable and effective for traffic data analysis.

Agentic Intelligence for Scalable Metadata Mining in Telecom Big Data Systems

期刊：Big Data · DOI：10.1177/2167647x261498697 · 摘要来源：Crossref 元数据

The high rate at which telecommunication services are changing has yielded a volume of heterogeneous data never seen before such as call detail records, signaling data, and subscriber activity traces. These datasets are crucial in relation to optimization and business intelligence, but effective use of such datasets requires quality metadata. Traditional metadata management is mostly manual and centralized, which cannot support the pace and scale of the new telecom big data systems. As a solution to these shortcomings, the present article suggests a scalable metadata mining strategy that is founded on agentic intelligence. In comparison to more traditional machine learning pipelines, agentic intelligence enables the use of autonomous goal-oriented AI agents, capable of reasoning in a hard data space and relevant to new network topologies and network schemas. The proposed architecture will include a multiagent architecture at a distributed level, having specialized agents: (1) schema drifting ingestion and monitoring agents of data flows; (2) semantic mining actors based on natural language processing to infer contextual metadata; (3) process-aware agents for process mining to determine lineage and dependencies between data; and (4) compliance with policy and metadata retrospect: government agents. A major effect of the work is the integration of agentic intelligence and scalable infrastructures of big data such as cloud-native analytics platforms and distributed data lakes. These agents coordinate themselves using lightweight coordination mechanisms to provide a persistent metadata knowledge layer that facilitates system interoperability and robust downstream analytics. The design concepts are geared toward horizontal scalability and fault tolerance, enabling the distribution of the agents at dynamically evolving manner regarding the workload peak. Besides, agents also enhance their strategies upon continuous feedback to handle concept drift as well. Typical examples of telecom applications such as monitoring network performance and fraud detection have demonstrated that such an approach far outperforms a rule-based approach in the accuracy, coverage, and timeliness of metadata. And lastly, the framework will be capable of offering more dynamic and sustainable data management within the telecom sector.
