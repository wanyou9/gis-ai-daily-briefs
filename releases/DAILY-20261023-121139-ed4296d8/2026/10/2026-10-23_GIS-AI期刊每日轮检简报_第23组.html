论文题名

Product Manifold Flow Matching for Mixed-Type Tabular Data Generation

期刊：Machine Learning · DOI：10.1007/s10994-026-07178-9 · 摘要来源：账本已存摘要

Synthetic tabular data generation is increasingly important for privacy-preserving machine learning in healthcare, finance, and other regulated industries. Many current methods embed categorical variables into continuous spaces, which can blur their discrete structure and introduce interpolations between categorical states. Here, we propose flow matching on product manifolds, where each variable type resides on its natural geometric space: continuous variables in Euclidean space, categorical variables on probability simplexes with Fisher-Rao geometry, and ordinal variables on order-preserving arcs of unit circles. Our framework employs coordinate-wise vector field decomposition for manifold-specific processing and geometry-aware midpoint aggregation to capture inter-type dependencies with preserved geometric consistency. We evaluate the method on 12 benchmark datasets that range from small clinical records to educational datasets with over 101 million samples. Results show measurable improvements in generation quality, particularly on categorical-heavy and large-scale datasets. Geometric constraints also exhibit empirical resistance to memorization without explicit noise injection, as measured by distance to the closest record and membership inference metrics. The coordinate-wise structure enables efficient parallel computation that scales well to large datasets.

Addressing Methodological Challenges in Time Series Anomaly Detection

期刊：Machine Learning · DOI：10.1007/s10994-026-07183-y · 摘要来源：账本已存摘要

Time series anomaly detection focuses on detecting unexpected events in time series data. Traditional techniques for this task include SVMs, k -Nearest Neighbors and PCA, while more recent approaches frequently employ deep learning, e.g., using variational autoencoders and graph neural networks. In several studies, these more elaborate techniques are regularly found to outperform traditional approaches. We present an in-depth review of 18 influential papers on time series anomaly detection using deep learning and found recurring methodological issues which lead to questionable or inconclusive results; leakage, modified datasets, and unclear baselines. We illustrate the importance of these issues with a novel empirical investigation and demonstrate it is not trivial to replicate reported relative performance of classical and deep learning methods. We find that older methods are frequently shown to perform worse than our non optimized implementations. We fail to reject the assumption of similar performance between these older and state of the art deep learning approaches on standard benchmark datasets.

论文题名

RLAX: A Reinforcement Learning-Based Framework for Explaining Anomalous Data

期刊：Machine Learning · DOI：10.1007/s10994-026-07182-z · 摘要来源：账本已存摘要

Singling out the most informative features able to discriminate between normal and anomalous data is a formidable problem dealing with the enormous search space represented by the power set of the whole feature set. These features indeed encode the explanation of the deviating behavior associated with selected observations and represent the hidden knowledge of interest for the analyst in many applicative contexts, namely cybersecurity, bioinformatics, and green by AI, to cite a few. In this work, we present RLAX, a reinforcement learning-based algorithm detecting explanations from collections of high-dimensional data sets. The algorithm receives in input the unlabelled data collection of normal instances, a small set of labelled inlier examples, and a small set of anomalies to be explained. Separation between normal samples and anomalies is measured by means of a distance-based outlier score. The designed learning strategy thus induces a ranking among features that allows isolating those maximizing separation accuracy. Beyond an add-only formulation, we investigate a subspace refinement strategy that augments the action space with remove and stop actions, enabling non-monotone refinement and adaptive early termination. Experimental results highlight that the size of the explanation returned consists of a small fraction of the whole feature set, and that the associated accuracy and subspace margin are of remarkable quality. Additional analyses of the refinement strategy further clarify its impact on exploration efficiency and explanation quality.

论文题名

MMT: Achieving Exact Federated Unlearning with Improved Post-unlearning Performance

期刊：Machine Learning · DOI：10.1007/s10994-026-07117-8 · 摘要来源：账本已存摘要

Federated learning allows multiple clients to collaboratively train a shared global model by sharing only model updates, such as gradients or parameters, to a central server without sharing their private data. Even though the clients do not share their private data, their shared model updates across multiple communication rounds indirectly influence the global model. This indirect influence poses significant challenges in many real-life applications, where it is necessary to completely remove a specific client’s influence (exact unlearning) from the global model. For example, exact unlearning may be required to comply with privacy regulations such as the GDPR’s Right to be Forgotten, or to remove the influence of malicious or poisoned clients in security-sensitive applications (e.g., jointly trained fraud detection models), where eliminating their influence is necessary to maintain the model’s performance. While retraining the global model with the remaining clients from scratch can achieve exact unlearning, it inevitably introduces significant deployment delays and leads to severe performance degradation due to random initialization, making it impractical for time-sensitive applications. To overcome these limitations, this paper proposes two exact federated unlearning algorithms, Bi-Models Training (bmt) and Multi-Models Training (mmt), that ensure immediate exact unlearning by design while achieving better performance post-unlearning. bmt preserves isolated local model copies during training and uses them to provide a stronger initialization for the global FL model after unlearning. To capture the joint influence of multiple clients, mmt trains a carefully constructed set of sub-FL models on disjoint client subsets to avoid overlapping influence, and then aggregates the most influential unaffected models to reinitialize the global FL model. Our experiments on vision and language tasks demonstrate that bmt consistently outperforms existing exact unlearning baselines, while mmt achieves over 2× improvement in post-unlearning performance, all while maintaining exact unlearning guarantees and compatibility with arbitrary federated learning protocols.

We present KARAMEL, a framework for multimodal entity linking (MEL)—the task of aligning visual and textual mentions to entities in a knowledge base—in the cultural heritage domain. Unlike prior vision–language models that primarily rely on surface-level multimodal alignment, our approach incorporates sociohistorical and contextual knowledge through a knowledge-aware architecture that fuses a vision–language encoder with a heterogeneous graph neural network. Grid-based Decision Tree Learning

Decision tree learning algorithms, such as Classification and Regression Trees (CART), are commonly founded on heuristics that greedily maximize purity gain. Despite their practical success, the theoretical properties of these algorithms, such as consistency, remain unclear. This paper uncovers a substantial obstacle in analyzing the consistency of decision tree learning algorithms, in which the worst-case purity gain can be zero and a gain value of zero usually results in a non-convergent training error. Experiments on 20 OpenML datasets show that the grid-based ensembles perform comparably to their conventional counterparts.

TACCO: Actor–Critic Contrastive Optimization for OOD Action Mitigation in Offline Reinforcement Learning

期刊：Machine Learning · DOI：10.1007/s10994-026-07170-3 · 摘要来源：账本已存摘要

Offline Reinforcement Learning provides a viable solution for tasks where online interaction is costly or infeasible by leveraging pre-collected data for policy learning. However, in an offline setting, the policy improvement process is prone to value overestimation due to distribution shifts, which can lead to policy degradation. Existing methods primarily address this issue through policy constraints or conservative value regularization, but few explicitly model the problem actions from a representation learning perspective. This paper proposes TACCO (TD3+BC with Actor-Critic Contrastive Optimization), a contrastive offline reinforcement learning method based on the TD3+BC framework, designed to address the out-of-distribution (OOD) action problem in offline reinforcement learning. We define OOD actions as those that lack local data support in a given state, do not match the current state, or exhibit low reliability. Furthermore, for dense and sparse reward environments, we design two distinct contrastive learning policies: TACCO-A performs contrastive learning on the actor side between data-driven actions, random actions, and cross-state policy actions; TACCO-B filters reliable samples on the critic side based on high Q-values and low uncertainty, and applies contrastive constraints to both reliable and remaining samples simultaneously. In this paper, we compare TACCO with six state-of-the-art offline reinforcement learning algorithms on the Mujoco and Adroit environments within the D4RL benchmark. Experimental results demonstrate that TACCO achieves superior performance on most tasks, validating its effectiveness in mitigating OOD action interference and enhancing policy stability.

Beyond Lipschitz: generalization and optimization analysis for adversarial training under Hölder-type conditions

期刊：Machine Learning · DOI：10.1007/s10994-026-07181-0 · 摘要来源：账本已存摘要

Adversarial training improves model robustness but may exhibit robust overfitting. Previous theoretical analyses mostly emphasize Lipschitz continuity of the adversarial loss, while understanding under weaker smoothness conditions (often observed in practice) remains limited. This paper investigates this problem in adversarial training, focusing on the generalization and optimization analyses of adversarial losses under weaker smoothness. We introduce Hölder continuity for the adversarial loss and propose a new concept of approximate Hölder smoothness, extending classical analyses from Lipschitz assumptions to a more general regime. A stability analysis for adversarial training is then developed, forming the theoretical basis for analyzing generalization and optimization. Under these conditions, we establish upper bounds on the generalization error and optimization gap of stochastic gradient descent for both convex and non-convex adversarial losses, and analyze the induced excess risk. These findings apply to a broad class of non-Lipschitz but Hölder smooth losses in deep learning, and offer theoretical insights into how optimization and generalization interact in adversarial training, contributing to a systematic understanding of robust overfitting. Furthermore, we empirically validate our theoretical findings using a practical example, demonstrating that the framework accurately captures generalization behavior under adversarial training.

论文题名

SURGE: Structural Perturbation Response Field Guided Residual Distillation for Graph Unlearning

期刊：Machine Learning · DOI：10.1007/s10994-026-07176-x · 摘要来源：账本已存摘要

We introduce SURGE, a graph unlearning framework that treats deletion requests as prediction-level structural perturbations. Graph unlearning requires removing requested nodes, edges, or features from a trained graph neural network (GNN) without full retraining while preserving utility on retained data. Existing approaches either disrupt graph structure through partitioning, approximate removal in parameter space, or rely on fixed neighborhoods that cannot express heterogeneous node sensitivity. SURGE addresses this limitation with a Sense–Repair–Distill pipeline: it estimates a continuous response field over all nodes, solves graph-regularized corrected logits without backpropagation, and transfers the corrected knowledge through response-adaptive distillation. Environmental Knowledge-Driven Transfer Strategy With Collaborative Learning for Dynamic Constrained Multiobjective Optimization

期刊：IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation · DOI：10.1109/tevc.2025.3643205 · 摘要来源：账本已存摘要

Abstract: In recent years, transfer-learning-based dynamic constrained multiobjective optimization algorithms (TL-DCMOAs) have shown great promise in handling dynamic constrained multiobjective optimization problems (DCMOPs). In existing TL-DCMOAs, environmental similarity is typically assessed by measures based on Euclidean distance. However, such measures merely capture overall numerical differences between two environments, which are sensitive to local fluctuations in environmental information, potentially leading to misjudgments of environmental similarity. Besides, most existing TL-DCMOAs leverage historical constrained and unconstrained Pareto-optimal solutions (UPSs) as knowledge, but neglect relations and implicit solutions behind them for acquiring knowledge. To address the above issues, an environmental knowledge-driven transfer strategy with collaborative learning (EKTS-CL) is proposed in this article. Specifically, we design two structural similarity measures (SSMs) which consider the mean, variance, and covariance of environmental information, to comprehensively assess the environmental structural discrepancies with respect to concentration, divergence, and correlation, respectively. Using SSMs, the most structurally similar historical UPSs and constrained Pareto-optimal solutions (CPSs) can be obtained. Additionally, true CPS and UPS share partial solutions across all environments or the majority of environments of a DCMOP. From the obtained CPS and UPS, that separately approximate the true CPS and UPS of the current environment, we extract implicit shared solutions and exclusive ones through collaborative learning, providing high-quality knowledge than the obtained plain solutions for knowledge transfer. Our experimental results include comprehensive comparisons with seven state-of-the-art DCMOAs, and show that the proposed EKTS-CL brings significant performance improvements in solving DCMOPs.

Abstract: This article devises a multimodal ACO (MACO) framework to solve multisolution traveling salesman problems (MSTSPs), which aim to find multiple optimal routes in a single run. Specifically, a dynamic niching strategy is designed to partition the ant colony into exclusive niches based on the route similarity measured by the common edges between two routes. Subsequently, MACO maintains a pheromone matrix for each niche and lets the ants in each niche construct their routes independently based on the associated pheromone matrix. For effective pheromone update, this article designs a niche and pheromone matrix association rule to match each newly partitioned niche with one pheromone matrix. To further improve optimization efficiency, this article designs a Dynamic Critical Edge Strategy to dig out critical edges during the optimization. Then, these critical edges are cooperated with 2-opt to refine the route constructed by each ant. To preserve the found optimal routes, this article devises an optimal route preservation strategy to store as many different high-quality routes as possible. Lastly, we instantiate MACO with four canonical ACO algorithms, namely Ant System(AS), Elite AS(EAS), Rank-based AS(ASRank), and Max-Min AS(MMAS), and then conduct extensive experiments to compare them with six state-of-the-art algorithms on the only one MSTSP benchmark suite and the TSPLIB instances. The experimental results reveal that the four MACOs perform competitively with one latest algorithm, but significantly outperform the other five comparison algorithms. Further analysis discovers that ASRank is the most effective one among the four ant colony optimizations (ACOs) to cooperate with MACO in solving MSTSPs.

Abstract: Solving constrained multiobjective optimization problems (CMOPs) involves simultaneously achieving convergence to the Pareto front, preserving solution diversity, and satisfying complex constraints. To address these challenges, this article proposes a multicriteria game-driven constrained multiobjective evolutionary algorithm (MGCMOEA), which leverages the complementary roles of a main population and an auxiliary population to jointly explore both feasible and infeasible regions. The algorithm introduces a refined feasibility classification mechanism that differentiates weakly feasible solutions from infeasible ones, thereby accelerating convergence while preserving potentially valuable solutions. To enhance cooperation between populations, MGCMOEA incorporates a multicriteria game-theoretic selection model that integrates dynamic game theory with the technique for order preference by similarity to ideal solution (TOPSIS). This model improves the guidance provided by the auxiliary population during evolution. Furthermore, to overcome inefficiencies associated with static population sizes, a dynamic resource allocation strategy is adopted, enabling adaptive distribution of computational resources throughout the evolutionary process. Experimental results on 47 benchmark functions and 12 real-world CMOPs demonstrate that MGCMOEA consistently outperforms 8 state-of-the-art algorithms in terms of convergence, diversity, and feasibility.

Abstract: Generalization is the core objective when training optimizers from data. However, limited training instances often constrain the generalization capability of the trained optimizers. Co-evolutionary approaches address this challenge by simultaneously evolving a parallel algorithm portfolio (PAP) and an instance population to eventually obtain PAPs with good generalization. Yet, when applied to a specific problem class, these approaches have a major limitation. They require practitioners to provide instance generators specially tailored to the problem class, which is often nontrivial to design. This work proposes a general-purpose, off-the-shelf PAP construction approach, named domain-agnostic co-evolution of parameterized search (DACE), for binary optimization problems where decision variables take values of 0 or 1. The key novelty of DACE lies in its neural network-based domain-agnostic instance representation and generation mechanism that eliminates the need for domain-specific instance generators. The strong generality of DACE is validated across three real-world binary optimization problems: 1) the complementary influence maximization problem (CIMP); 2) the compiler arguments optimization problem (CAOP); and 3) the contamination control problem (CCP). Given only a small set of training instances from these problem classes, DACE, without requiring domain knowledge, constructs PAPs with even better generalization performance than existing approaches on all three classes, despite their use of domain-specific instance generators.

Abstract: Parametric multiobjective optimization (PMO) addresses the challenge of solving an infinite number of multiobjective optimization problems (MOPs), where optimal solutions must adapt to varying parameters. The experimental results demonstrate that PPSL significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of solution quality and adaptability. Importantly, we emphasize that PPSL is not limited to the two problems tested in this work; it is broadly applicable to any real-world problem that can be modeled as a PMO problem, offering a versatile and powerful solution for a wide range of applications. Abstract: Many large-scale combinatorial optimization problems (LSCOPs) are challenging to solve due to their high dimensionalities and complex search spaces. To deal with tens of thousands of decision variables, problem reduction approaches have proven effective in reducing the original problem instance to a more manageable size, thereby decreasing the dimensionality of problem instances before optimization. However, since the dimensionality of transformed problem instances remains fixed during the optimization process, the effectiveness of existing methods is highly dependent on the selected features. In this article, instead of only transforming the original problem instance once prior to the optimization, we propose a novel learning-based adaptive problem reduction (LAPR) framework to facilitate solving LSCOPs. Based on a learning model, the LAPR framework initially transforms the original problem instance into a low-dimensional one, and then dynamically increases the problem dimensionality when necessary during the optimization. To evaluate the effectiveness and efficiency of our framework, we have applied it to solve uncapacitated facility location problems (UFLPs) using three effective features to construct a machine learning model learning from small instances. Based on this model, the LAPR framework first identifies a subset of the most promising facilities to construct an initial reduced problem instance through a novel optimal-k estimation strategy. Then, it incrementally incorporates potentially promising facilities into candidate solutions during the evolutionary process to improve the optimization performance. Extensive experimental studies on a series of large-scale UFLP benchmarks demonstrate that our proposed LAPR framework significantly enhances the performance of existing solution methods and consistently outperforms state-of-the-art problem reduction frameworks.

Multi-granularity spatiotemporal flow patterns

期刊：Geoinformatica · DOI：10.1007/s10707-026-00592-0 · 摘要来源：账本已存摘要

Analyzing flow of objects or data at different granularities of space and time can unveil interesting insights or trends. For example, transportation companies, by aggregating passenger travel data (e.g., counting passengers traveling from one region to another), can analyze movement behavior. In this paper, we study the problem of finding important trends in passenger movements between regions at different granularities. We define Origin ( O ), Destination ( D ), and Time ( T ) patterns (ODT patterns) and propose a bottom-up algorithm that enumerates them. We suggest and employ optimizations that greatly reduce the search space and the computational cost of pattern enumeration. We also propose pattern variants (constrained patterns and top- k patterns) that could be useful to different applications scenarios. Finally, we propose an approximate solution that fast identifies ODT patterns of specific sizes, following a generate-and-test approach. We evaluate the efficiency and effectiveness of our methods on three real datasets and showcase interesting ODT flow patterns in them.

论文题名

Weighted UnLearning from Experience for Improved Spurious Correlation Robustness

期刊：Machine Learning · DOI：10.1007/s10994-026-07164-1 · 摘要来源：账本已存摘要

Despite their success, Deep Learning (DL) models are susceptible to spurious correlations. These are accidental correlations between non-predictive features of the training dataset and the target label, leading to models that do not generalise well to samples without such spurious correlations. We propose to address this failure mode by extending and improving an existing method for spurious correlation robustness — UnLearning from Experience (ULE). ULE trains two models in parallel; a biased model is encouraged to learn the spurious correlations, while a separate debiased model is trained to solve the same task while avoiding learning the same behaviour as the first, thus avoiding learning the spurious correlations. A weakness of ULE is that it treats every training example identically, regardless of whether or not it contains a spurious correlation. Therefore, ULE does not make optimal use of the training data, leading to worse-than-necessary performance. To address this weakness, we introduce Weighted UnLearning from Experience (wULE), which modifies the original ULE loss function to reweight the components depending on whether a training sample likely contains spurious correlations. Hence, we explore two methods for identifying training samples likely to contain spurious correlations. We test our approach using several well-known spurious correlation robustness benchmarks — Waterbirds, CelebA, UrbanCars and MultiNLI. Our novel wULE method outperforms the original ULE in all cases and achieves Worst-Group Accuracy (WGA) results comparable to the best methods in the spurious correlation robustness literature.

论文题名

The Challenges of Hyperparameter Tuning for Accurate Causal Effect Estimation

期刊：Machine Learning · DOI：10.1007/s10994-026-07140-9 · 摘要来源：账本已存摘要

ML is playing an increasingly crucial role in estimating causal effects of treatments on outcomes from observational data. Many ML methods (‘causal estimators’) have been proposed for this task. All of these methods, as with any ML approach, require extensive hyperparameter tuning. For non-causal predictive tasks, there is a consensus on the choice of tuning metrics (e.g. mean squared error), making it simple to compare models. However, for causal inference tasks, such a consensus is yet to be reached, making a any comparison of causal models difficult. On top of that, there is no ideal metric on which to tune causal estimators, so one must rely on proxies. Furthermore, the fact that model selection in causal inference involves multiple components (causal estimator, ML regressor, hyperparameters, metric), complicates the issue even further. In order to evaluate the importance of each component, we perform an extensive empirical study on their combination. Our experimental setup involves many commonly used causal estimators, regressors (‘base learners’ henceforth) and metrics applied to four well-known causal inference benchmark datasets. Our results show that hyperparameter tuning increased the probability of reaching state-of-the-art performance in average ( 65% → 81% ) and individualised ( 50% → 57% ) effect estimation with only commonly used estimators. We also show that the performance of standard metrics can be inconsistent across different scenarios. Our findings highlight the need for further research to establish whether metrics uniformly capable of state-of-the-art performance in causal model evaluation can be found.
